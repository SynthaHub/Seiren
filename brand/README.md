them, and do not reference them from the app.** The site serves derived files
from `public/brand/`, generated by:

```
python scripts/brand.py
```

## What the site uses, and why

The chrome uses the **horizontal** lockup. The stacked cuts are near-square
(ratio 0.92) and would either tower over the 72px header bar or shrink the name
to nothing; horizontal is 2.98:1 and sits in that bar at a readable size.
Switching to stacked is a one-line change in `scripts/brand.py`.

- Light grounds → `lockup_horizontal_primary.png`
- `.on-navy` grounds → `lockup_horizontal_reversed.png`

## Three things to know before touching these

**Every "white" and "reversed" file has an opaque navy ground baked in.** They
are not white-on-transparent. Dropped into a page unprocessed they paint a navy
rectangle over whatever they sit on. The reversed lockup is used *with* its
ground, because that navy is `#1B2A4A` — byte-identical to `--seiran-navy` — so
it composites invisibly on any `.on-navy` band and its edges stay antialiased
against the exact colour they land on. **If the navy token ever changes, these
files must be re-exported or a seam will appear.**

**The primary lockup sets PARTNERS in green, `#2F4B3C`.** SPES-001 documents no
green anywhere. It is recorded in `src/styles/theme.css` as `--seiran-green` so
nobody "corrects" it to gold. The reversed cut uses Gold Leaf instead, so the
two variants genuinely differ in more than lightness.

**The reversed files carry compression noise at the canvas edge** — a border of
pixels that are near-navy but not exactly navy. Any alpha-keying must threshold
it away, or a bounding box comes back as almost the whole canvas. `ALPHA_FLOOR`
in `scripts/brand.py` handles this.
