## The name - and why it is the design brief

"Seiran" comes from a Japanese expression for **a clear sky after a storm**. The storm is the
uncertainty and pressure an organisation faces as it grows; the clear sky is the clarity,
stronger leadership, and sustainable growth on the other side.

**Seiran exists to help organisations move from complexity to clarity.**

This is the most valuable thing in the entire brief, and it is the axis the design should turn
on. Most advisory firms have no idea of this kind - they have a name, a blue palette, and a
stock photograph. Seiran has a concrete visual metaphor with a built-in *before and after*, and
a directional movement from turbulence to calm.

That metaphor can live in structure, in how a page resolves as it scrolls, in the relationship
between a dense area and an open one, in light. It should **not** be taken literally as
weather photography, cloud illustrations, or a sky-blue gradient - the literal reading is the
obvious one, which makes it the one every viewer discounts. Look for the structural reading.

Restraint matters here: the metaphor is strongest used once, well. Applied to every section it
becomes a motif, and a motif is decoration.

## Purpose, vision, mission

**Purpose** - To help organizations build stronger, more capable and more resilient
institutions, creating lasting value for owners, employees, customers, communities and future
generations.

**Vision** - A future where owner-managed businesses become enduring institutions that create
lasting value for people, communities, and generations.

**Mission** - To partner with owner-managed businesses to build clarity, strengthen leadership,
design effective systems, execute meaningful strategies, and achieve sustainable transformation.

**Core promise** - Seiran helps owner-managed businesses turn business complexity into clarity,
stronger execution and sustainable growth.

**Brand story** - Businesses grow and complexity grows with them. What worked when an
organisation was small stops working as it scales, and the founder cannot keep carrying
everything. Seiran represents the transition from complexity to clarity: understanding what is
happening, identifying what is holding a business back, clarifying the way forward, and building
the capability required for sustainable growth. The goal is not a bigger organisation - it is an
enduring one.

## Who is actually reading this site

An owner-manager of a Kenyan SME - retail, wholesale, food and hospitality, professional
services, agribusiness, private education - or a leader in a faith-based or mission-driven
organisation. Defined not by size or sector but by this: **the owner is still carrying too
much, and growth has made that unsustainable.**

They are pragmatic, time-poor, and sceptical of consultants who deliver a report and leave.
They are likely on a phone. They have probably been burned by, or heard about, an expensive
deck that changed nothing.

Design implications that follow directly:

- **Substance over polish.** This reader trusts specificity - the four pillars with their real
  sub-services listed - more than a beautifully vague value proposition.
- **The site must feel like the firm.** An advisory firm selling clarity and strong systems
  cannot have a cluttered, slow, or confusing website. The site is the first proof of the claim,
  and a visitor will read it that way whether or not they do so consciously.
- **Founder-led credibility.** One named consultant with real experience and a network of
  associates. Do not design for a 200-person firm; design for a credible specialist practice.
  Pretending to more scale than exists is the fastest way to lose this reader.
- **Mobile and mid-tier Android, on 4G.** Performance is a trust signal here, not a metric.

## Voice

Plain, direct, and confident without inflation. The brief's own line sets the register:

> *A report is not the finish line. Transformation is.*

That is the voice - short, declarative, slightly contrarian about the consulting industry, and
about outcomes rather than method.

**Do:** name the reader's actual situation in their words ("you are still approving every
purchase"). Use concrete nouns. Let the four pillars and their sub-services do the persuading.
Write the FAQ answers as straight answers.

**Do not:** use "leverage", "synergy", "solutions", "empower", "unlock", "seamless",
"cutting-edge", or "bespoke". Do not open with "In today's fast-paced business environment".
Do not invent statistics, client counts, or testimonials - **there are no case studies yet, and
the brief is explicit that none may be published without client permission.** Fabricated proof
would be self-defeating for a firm whose product is honest diagnosis.

## The inspiration references

Three Webflow templates were supplied as direction: **Vistara** (business consulting, "clean and
confident, built to establish trust at first glance"), **Humace** (HR and recruitment consulting,
SEO-structured), **Axentix** (business consulting, "clean layout, structured content sections,
sophisticated visual hierarchy").

They agree on a direction, and it is a sound one for this audience:

**Take from them** - generous whitespace and calm density; clear structural hierarchy that makes
a services page easy to navigate; substantial content sections instead of teaser fragments;
trust built through clarity rather than decoration; a CMS-shaped Insights hub; strong SEO
structure; visible, repeated primary CTA.

**Leave behind** - they are, by construction, templates: built to fit any consultancy, which
means nothing in them is specific to one. The shared skeleton (centered hero with two buttons →
three feature cards → stats bar → testimonial carousel → CTA), the stock imagery of handshakes
and glass towers, the invented metrics, and the corporate-blue default all come from needing to
suit every buyer at once.

Seiran does not have that constraint. **The templates set the level of craft and calm to hit;
the name, the story, and the reader supply what makes it Seiran's.** When those two pull in
different directions, the brand foundation above wins - that is where the distinctiveness comes
from, and a client who has read the brief will recognise its absence immediately.

## Content Seiran has not supplied yet

Do not invent any of these. Where a design needs them, use a clearly labelled placeholder and
list it as outstanding:

- Email, telephone, WhatsApp number
- Live social channels (LinkedIn company page; founder LinkedIn is known)
- Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Cookie notice, copyright line
- Final domain
- Any case study whatsoever
- Photography of the founder or the workspace
